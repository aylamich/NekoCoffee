# TESTS — Neko Cafe (=^.^=)

How the tests were done:

- **Tests per function:** every business function was called **by importing the module** (`import neko_coffee`), without the menu. The **Expected** values were calculated by hand from the business rules BR01–BR11; the **Actual** values are the real output of the calls (the tables were generated by a script that calls the functions, never typed by hand).
- **Built-in test mode (menu option 7):** `run_tests()` runs 65 checks → **65/65 passed**.
- **End-to-end run:** the script was run with `python neko_coffee.py < inputs.txt`, covering every scenario listed in section 2.

Summary: **121/121 cases in the tables below passed**, plus 65/65 in the test mode.

Highlighted boundary values: minutes **29/30/59/60** (discount), energy **41/40/16/15** (status), minutes **4/5/120/121** and **15/16** for a tired cat (validation), hunger **79/80**, happiness **21/20**, energy **16/15** (alerts).

## 3. Simulated visit sequences (rule check)

New cat (hunger 30, happiness 70, energy 80), real output of `apply_visit_effect`:

| Sequence | Result | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| A: visits of 60 min | 80 → 60 (Available) → 40 (Tired) → next 60-min visits blocked | After 2 h in a row the cat asks for a break |
| B: visits of 30 min | 80 → 70 → 60 → 50 → 40 (Tired), then only 15 min | 2 h of visits before getting tired |
| C: 120 min, then 15 min | 80 → 40 (Tired) → 35 → 30 → 25 → 20 | A tired cat still accepts short visits and does not reach "Resting" too fast |

Conclusion: with BR06 (−1 energy every 3 min), a rested cat handles about 2 h of visits before becoming "Tired", and each nap (+40) gives back about 2 h. The values make sense for a working day at the cafe.
